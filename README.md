# Tokens

Reusable glTF pieces. Original commissioned artwork is preserved in each
asset's `source` directory as bake input, not as current export geometry.
Current dice meshes are generated by `scripts/assets/regular-dice.py` into
`regular/`; published models and maps live beside those directories. Asset preparation requires ImageMagick with
AVIF and WebP support.

`src/assets/catalog.json` lists the published pieces.

Assets were created by Misha Tsyatsko under contract for Garbo Succus.

- `pnpm prepare-assets` rebuilds generated assets.
- `pnpm build` validates the catalog and writes `dist`.
- `pnpm dev` builds and starts the catalog preview.
- `pnpm build-storybook` builds the deployable preview.

## Regular dice and face poses

All seven dice models (including the classic pip cube) use centred, symmetric meshes with a **1.2 mm geometric fillet radius at catalog scale**. Faces
are flat; edges are cylindrical and corners spherical, with analytic smooth normals. D10/D12/D20 use one subdivision
per half-edge/corner patch; the sharper shapes use two. UV islands use a spacious painting grid: one upright island per numbered
face, including its rounded rim. Seams meet halfway around rounded edges;
there are no separate edge/corner islands or overlapping triangles. Classic pips are transferred onto the new UVs; numbered faces use the bundled
Comic Neue Bold font. Engraving is rebuilt
from the final ink. Blank versions share the exact same mesh.

Original artwork remains in `source`; reproducible bake outputs are in `regular`.
Rebuild with Blender 5, Pillow, NumPy and SciPy available:

```sh
for die in die d4 d6 d8 d12 d20 d10; do
  blender -b --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/assets/regular-dice.py -- "$die"
done
node scripts/prepare-assets.mjs
```

The optional names limit image conversion; omitting them rebuilds all numbered
assets. Preparation also synchronizes blank geometry and generates face metadata.
All blank glTFs share their numbered variant's published `.bin`, including
positions, normals, UVs, tangents and indices. `sync-blank-dice.py` is the single
blank-model generator; all blanks share one neutral normal-map texel.
`blank-dice.py` is a compatibility entry point for this same generator.
D10 is a pentagonal trapezohedron with ten congruent kite faces, numbered 0–9
(0 represents ten). Opposite numbers sum to nine. `d10_geometry.py` defines its
planes and exact rotational symmetries; outcomes use 18-degree yaw increments
and zero Z rotation, preserving the full silhouette. All numbered dice use
`scripts/assets/dice_font.py` and the same bundled Comic Neue Bold font,
including matching 6/9 dots. The numbered and blank D10 share one
optimized 220-triangle mesh: one subdivision per half-edge, with additional
radial subdivisions only at the two five-face tips to preserve their rounding.

D8/D12/D20 outcomes are fitted to the mesh planes and the whole solid's normal
symmetries, preserving the original number mapping and a stable footprint.
`authored-dice-poses.json` retains the original hints for identifying numbered
faces. D4 uses exact tetrahedral rotations. Classic die and D6 poses are unchanged.
Faces are embedded in each glTF as well as the shared catalog, so imported models
retain their roll outcomes without a separate catalog.

```sh
python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts/assets
node scripts/build-catalog.mjs
```

The tests require NumPy, Pillow 11.3+ with AVIF support, and SciPy, and cover all catalog outcomes, stable bounds,
resting planes, regular geometry, the shared physical fillet radius, and identical
numbered/blank model buffers.

## Coordinated physical sizes

`dice_style.py` defines the common style in millimetres. Nominal size is measured
vertically from a resting face: Classic/D4/D6/D8/D10 are 16 mm, D12 is 18 mm,
and D20 is 20 mm. These are deliberate design targets inspired by commercial
16/18/20 mm sets (https://www.hddice.com/), not exact Chessex measurements.
Different shapes naturally have different footprints at those heights.

The generator resizes support planes before constructing the 1.2 mm fillet;
scaling a finished rounded mesh would incorrectly change the physical radius.
Numbered and blank variants retain identical sizes, poses, geometry and UVs.

## Centered numeral artwork

Preparation measures numerals in their final reading orientation, so rotated UV
islands and 6/9 dots cannot displace the numeral itself. D4's three labels on each
face use identical corner spacing. Lettering uses a common physical height per
die. Digits and disambiguation dots move together. Engraved normal-map detail is
regenerated from the final ink so old print/engraving offsets cannot survive.
Every label has at least 0.45 mm of clearance inside the flat face polygon.
A single physical digit height is fitted to the widest label on each die,
including two-digit values and 6/9 dots. Height is capped at 7 mm and 60% of the
flat face's reading height; D4 uses its three corner anchors, a 4 mm cap and 27%.
The font uses 90% horizontal scale and an ink-to-ink digit gap of 4% of numeral
height, approximating the compact lettering in Chessex opaque-set photos.
These are visual targets, not exact measurements or the proprietary Chessex font.
References: https://gnomishbazaar.com/products/chessex-opaque-white-black-polyhedral-7-die-set-chx25401
and https://superretro.com.au/products/polyhedral-7-die-set-opaque-ivoryblack.

Engraving normals are regenerated after this fitting, with a 0.18 mm virtual
depth, a 0.25 mm rounded inward profile, and 0.04 mm rim smoothing converted
through each model's UV texel density. The groove curves across the numeral
stroke instead of producing a narrow lip around a flat bottom.
Mesh normals remain analytic and tangent frames are rebuilt for the new meshes;
blank models retain the shared neutral normal texel.

`centered/` maps are generated from the bundled font (or classic pip bake) by
`scripts/assets/center-dice-labels.py`, using NumPy, Pillow and SciPy. All dice
use analytic mesh normals with neutral blank maps, so old baked edges cannot
make the faces appear inset. Atlas padding is white to prevent dark island
borders bleeding into faces at coarse mip levels. Preparation runs this step
automatically; repeated builds never re-center an already processed image.

The unmodified installable font, SIL Open Font License, source revision and SHA-256
are in `src/assets/fonts/`, distributed once as `dist/fonts/`. Install the TTF in
Photoshop or another painting application to match the supplied artwork. The
font has its own OFL license; original commissioned source art retains its attribution.

Tests cover the published AVIFs as well as the PNG masters, physical reading-frame
anchors, equal lettering heights, complete digit/dot components, residual engraving,
engraving registration/direction, white filtering gutters, flat regular blank maps,
and rebuilding regular dice without stale published blank maps.

## Painting your own dice

Each die's `paint/` folder contains 2048×2048 PNGs: `blank.png`, `numbered.png`,
transparent `uv-guide.png`, and a labelled `reference.png`. Open the blank or
numbered image in Photoshop (or another image editor), add the guide as a layer,
paint underneath it, then hide the guide before exporting. Keep the original
square dimensions and extend colours beyond island outlines for filtering.

Faces are arranged in number order, upright in their reading frame. Each island
includes its rounded rim, so painting a face also colours its edges. Artwork is
never forced to repeat. D4 labels identify the vertex opposite each physical face.
Use a neutral normal map for custom art to avoid retaining the old engraving.
`paint/README.txt` explains the layers and seam matching in more detail.

The standalone `dist/` build includes every die’s `paint/` layers and instructions.

## Lossless mesh optimization

`optimize_gltf.py` welds vertices only when every attribute byte matches,
including UVs, normals, and tangents. It retains separate vertices at painting
seams, preserves triangle winding and topology, orders triangles for a small
vertex cache, and orders vertex data by first use. No quantization, geometry
simplification, or runtime decoder is involved. Generation and preparation both
apply it; repeated runs are idempotent. Tests compare the full attribute data
of every oriented triangle before and after optimization.

Byte-identical published buffers are shared across numbered models too; Classic,
D6 and blank D6 resolve to the same cube buffer. Bake inputs remain self-contained.
